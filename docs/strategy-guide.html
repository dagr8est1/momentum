Momentum Investing Strategy

A cross-sectional + time-series momentum stock-rotation strategy, backtested with backtrader and packaged as an installable, config-driven Python tool. This doc explains what the strategy does and why, then how the codebase is put together — enough to walk through either half in an interview.

1Strategy Overview & Rationale

The strategy rotates a fixed universe of large, liquid names into a small, equal-conviction book of trending winners — while stepping fully to cash whenever the broad market itself is in a downtrend. It combines three well-known ideas from the momentum literature rather than inventing a new signal.

Philosophy

Cross-sectional

Rank stocks against each other every period; only hold the strongest relative performers (top 5).

Time-series

Each holding must also be trending up in absolute terms (price above its own 200-day average) — this is "dual momentum" (Antonacci): relative and absolute trend must agree.

Regime-aware

A market-level trend filter (benchmark vs. its 200-day average) can flatten the entire book to cash — the strategy doesn't try to pick winners in a falling market.

Universe

configs/default.yaml defines a static list of ~140 tickers: mega-cap single names across sectors (tech, healthcare, financials, industrials, staples, energy), sector SPDRs (XLK, XLF, …), factor/style ETFs (MTUM, QUAL, VLUE, USMV), thematic ETFs (ARKK, ICLN, SOXX), international/EM (EFA, EEM, FXI), and bonds/commodities (TLT, GLD, USO). Broad diversity of asset classes means momentum can rotate into whatever regime is actually working — equities, gold, bonds — not just equities.

The universe is pluggable: source: static uses the inline ticker list; source: index:sp500 instead scrapes current S&P 500 constituents from Wikipedia at run time (universe.py).

Regime filter (portfolio-level)

Every bar, the benchmark's close (default SPY) is compared to its own 200-day simple moving average. If the benchmark is below that average, every open position is closed and the strategy holds cash until the benchmark recovers above it. This is the "time-series momentum" half of dual momentum applied at the market level — it's the mechanism that keeps the strategy out of prolonged bear markets rather than fighting them stock-by-stock.

Stock-level trend filter

Within a bullish regime, a stock is only eligible if its own close is above its 200-day SMA (ts_mom_lookback). This is a cheap absolute-momentum gate applied before any relative scoring — a stock ranking well on momentum but trading below its own long-term trend is excluded outright.

Scoring model (cross-sectional rank)

Eligible stocks (passed the trend filter, positive momentum) get a blended score combining three signals, each computed by a pure function in scoring.py:

SignalFunctionWhat it measures
Momentum blend momentum_blend(closes, [60,120,252]) Mean price change over three horizons (~3 mo / ~6 mo / ~1 yr) vs. today — smooths out any single lookback's noise.
Frog-in-the-Pan (FIP) fip_score(returns) Fraction of the last 252 days that closed up. Rewards steady, "boring" grinding uptrends over the same total return achieved via a few violent jumps — the academic finding is that gradual-information moves continue more reliably than jump-driven ones.
Skewness skewness_score(closes) Skew of the daily log-return distribution over the last 90 days.

Combined score

score = momentum_weight   * momentum_blend
      + fip_weight        * fip_score
      + skewness_penalty  * skewness_score

Default weights: momentum_weight=0.5, fip_weight=0.5, skewness_penalty=0.5. Stocks are ranked by this score and the top 5 are selected.

Naming vs. behavior — worth knowing cold Despite being called skewness_penalty, it's added to the score with a positive sign and a positive default weight — so under current defaults, more positively-skewed (jumpier, "lottery-like") return distributions score higher, not lower. If the intent were to genuinely penalize jump-driven moves (as the name suggests, and as some momentum-crash literature would argue), this coefficient would need to be negative. As shipped, it's a configurable tilt, not a penalty — flip the sign in config to actually discourage skew.

Position sizing & rebalancing

  • Sizing: inverse-volatility weighting (inverse_vol_weights) across the top 5 — lower-volatility names get proportionally larger allocations, a simple risk-parity tilt within the concentrated book. Zero/negative volatility is excluded from the normalization rather than dividing by zero.
  • Rebalance trigger: fires immediately if top-5 membership changes, or on a periodic schedule (default: monthly) even if membership is unchanged — the periodic trigger exists specifically so inverse-vol weights get refreshed as relative volatility drifts, even when the same 5 names keep winning.
  • Sizing safety buffer: target position values are sized against 99% of portfolio value, not 100% — backtrader's order_target_value sizes off today's close but fills at tomorrow's open, so a 1% buffer absorbs overnight drift and avoids spurious margin rejections on a fast-moving holding sized near 100%.

Explicit non-goals

This is a research/backtesting tool, not a trading system:

  • No live or paper-trading execution.
  • No walk-forward optimization or parameter search tooling.
  • No multi-strategy portfolio composition — this is a single strategy, single portfolio.

2Code Structure & How to Run It

The package was refactored from a single 300+ line script (raw/main.py, kept for reference) into a proper src-layout Python package with the trading math separated from the backtrader execution engine, so the math can be unit tested without spinning up a backtest.

Package layout

momentum/ ├── pyproject.toml, README.md ├── configs/ │ └── default.yaml # sample run config ├── src/momentum/ │ ├── config.py # RunConfig / StrategyConfig dataclasses + YAML loader │ ├── universe.py # static list or dynamic index-constituent resolution │ ├── data.py # yfinance fetch + local parquet cache │ ├── scoring.py # pure math: momentum, FIP, skewness, combined score, inverse-vol │ ├── indicators.py # backtrader Indicator wrappers around scoring.py │ ├── strategy.py # MomentumStrategy(bt.Strategy) │ ├── backtest.py # builds Cerebro, wires feeds + strategy, extracts returns │ ├── reporting.py # quantstats tearsheet generation │ └── cli.py # `momentum run configs/default.yaml` ├── tests/ # one test module per src module, plus a smoke test └── raw/ # original single-file prototype this was built from

Module reference

ModuleResponsibilityNotes
scoring.py Pure momentum/FIP/skewness/inverse-vol math Zero backtrader imports — importable and unit-testable standalone. This is the module to point to when asked "what's actually novel here."
indicators.py RollingSkewness and FrogInThePan, thin bt.Indicator subclasses Each just windows the price series and calls into scoring.py per bar — no math lives here.
strategy.py MomentumStrategy(bt.Strategy) — the regime filter, trend filter, scoring loop, and order placement The only place that touches backtrader's per-bar next() lifecycle and broker/position APIs.
config.py RunConfig / StrategyConfig dataclasses; YAML loader with validation Validates required top-level fields and rebalance_frequency (only None or "monthly") at load time — fails fast instead of at strategy runtime.
universe.py Resolves a ticker list from config source: static (inline list) or source: index:sp500 (live Wikipedia scrape).
data.py Per-ticker OHLCV fetch via yfinance, backed by a local parquet cache Cache is coverage-based: a cached file is reused only if its date range already covers the request; otherwise it re-fetches and merges. Also flattens the MultiIndex columns yfinance sometimes returns.
backtest.py Orchestration: builds Cerebro, adds benchmark as datas[0] and universe as datas[1:], runs, extracts the TimeReturn analyzer Missing per-ticker data is logged and skipped, not fatal; missing benchmark data is fatal (the regime filter can't function without it).
reporting.py One-line wrapper around quantstats.reports.html(...) Replaced a ~230-line hand-rolled Excel reporting pipeline in the original prototype.
cli.py argparse-based entrypoint: momentum run <config> [--output path] Registered as a console script (project.scripts in pyproject.toml), so it runs as momentum once installed.

Running it

# install dependencies
uv sync

# run a backtest against the default config
uv run momentum run configs/default.yaml

# write the tearsheet somewhere else
uv run momentum run configs/default.yaml --output results/tearsheet.html

# run the test suite
uv run pytest

A run downloads (and caches under .cache/) OHLCV data for every ticker in the universe plus the benchmark, executes the strategy over the configured date range, and writes an HTML performance tearsheet (default tearsheet.html) comparing the strategy's returns to the benchmark's.

Testing strategy

  • test_scoring.py — pure math: combined-score weighting, FIP ratio on known positive/negative-day sequences, skewness on a known return series, inverse-vol normalization including the zero-volatility edge case. No backtrader involved.
  • test_config.py — YAML loads into the expected dataclass shape; invalid/missing fields raise clear errors at load time.
  • test_backtest_smoke.py — a small end-to-end run (benchmark + a handful of tickers, short date range, no live network calls) asserting the pipeline completes, produces a non-empty returns series, and writes a tearsheet. This is the regression guard for cross-module wiring.
  • test_data.py, test_universe.py, test_indicators.py, test_strategy.py, test_cli.py, test_reporting.py — one module per source file, all network calls mocked/monkeypatched.

All 33 tests pass with uv run pytest; no test hits the network.

3Interview Prep

The points below are the ones most likely to come up if someone probes "why is it built this way" rather than just "what does it do."

Key design decisions

  • Scoring math is framework-agnostic. scoring.py has zero backtrader imports specifically so the strategy's core logic can be unit tested in milliseconds, without a full backtest run.
  • Dual rebalance trigger, added deliberately. The original prototype only rebalanced on membership change, which meant inverse-vol weights went stale for names that stayed in the top 5 for months. A monthly trigger was added alongside the membership-change trigger to keep sizing current.
  • Cache is coverage-based, not time-based. data.py re-fetches only when the cached date range doesn't cover what's requested — not on a TTL — so re-running the same backtest twice does zero network calls the second time.
  • Config validated at load time. Both missing required fields and an invalid rebalance_frequency raise immediately in config.py, rather than surfacing as a confusing failure deep inside a multi-minute backtest run.
  • Per-ticker data gaps degrade gracefully; benchmark gaps don't. A missing/empty ticker is logged and skipped — the backtest proceeds with the rest of the universe. A missing benchmark is a hard failure, because the regime filter has no fallback.

Gotchas worth knowing cold

1. skewness_penalty currently rewards, not penalizes, skew See the scoring section above — the coefficient is added with a positive sign and a positive default weight.
2. Sizing uses a 99%-of-value buffer Because order_target_value sizes against today's close but fills at the next bar's open, a position sized at exactly 100% of portfolio value can get margin-rejected on a single bar of price drift. The 1% reserve is a deliberate, documented workaround, not an arbitrary constant.
3. Only "monthly" is a valid non-null rebalance frequency today config.py enforces this explicitly; strategy.py's own _is_rebalance_due would otherwise raise a less-clear ValueError deep inside a run for any other string.

Questions you might get, and how to answer them

Why separate scoring.py from strategy.py?

Testability and reuse — the same momentum/FIP/skewness math could back a different execution engine or a research notebook without dragging in backtrader.

Why inverse-volatility sizing instead of equal weight?

It's a simple risk-parity tilt: among the top-5 momentum names, it reduces the influence of the single most volatile holding on portfolio swings, without needing a full covariance-based optimizer.

What happens in a sideways, choppy market?

The regime filter only reacts to the benchmark's 200-day trend, so a genuinely choppy-but-flat market won't trigger the cash switch — the strategy could still whipsaw in and out of individual names as membership changes, since there's no minimum holding period beyond the periodic/membership rebalance logic.

What would you change or add next?

Candidates: transaction-cost/slippage modeling (not currently simulated), walk-forward parameter validation (explicitly out of scope today), and resolving the skewness-sign question above with an actual empirical study rather than leaving it as a tunable default.
